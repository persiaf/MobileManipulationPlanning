   From Tabletop Tasks to Reachability-Aware
Docking Poses: A Hierarchical Planning Framework
       for Dual-Arm Mobile Manipulators

   Abstract—Multi-table mobile manipulation requires a robot
to determine not only how to navigate between workspaces but
also where to dock for feasible manipulation. Directly treating
tabletop tasks as navigation goals ignores the mismatch between
mobile-base configurations and arm workspaces, which may
lead to infeasible manipulation execution. This paper presents
RCH-MT-MMP, a reachability-constrained hierarchical planning
framework for multi-task dual-arm mobile manipulation. The
framework first partitions tabletop tasks into local regions
and generates collision-free docking candidates around tables.
Each candidate is evaluated considering task coverage, dual-
arm reachability, safety constraints, and approach feasibility, and
multiple docking poses are selected through a coverage-aware
strategy. The selected poses are then connected by a replaceable
global path planner.                                                                 (a) Experimental scene                 (b) Robot platform

Remark 1 (Approximation scope). The docking selection
can be viewed as a cost-aware set-cover problem, while the
routing stage resembles a traveling-salesman problem over
the selected docking configurations. This work does not claim
global optimality of the joint problem. Instead, it provides a
feasible hierarchical decomposition with finite termination and
explicit complexity characterization.

   Experiments are conducted using the MATLAB implemen-
tation of the proposed RCH-MT-MMP framework. The main
benchmark considers a tabletop-service environment with a
10 m × 10 m workspace containing 16 rectangular tables and
36 tabletop task points. The objective is to evaluate whether
the proposed framework can transform tabletop manipulation
requirements into feasible docking decisions and subsequently
